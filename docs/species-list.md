# Cetacean species reference

Species with regular or recurring presence in Norwegian Sea, Barents Sea, North Sea, Svalbard waters and the Russian Arctic edge of the European Arctic. Vagrants are noted separately.

Categories used:

- **IUCN**: LC (Least Concern), NT (Near Threatened), VU (Vulnerable), EN (Endangered), CR (Critically Endangered), DD (Data Deficient).
- **Norwegian Red List 2021** (Artsdatabanken): same letters plus NA (not applicable, used for vagrants and irregular visitors that do not reproduce in Norwegian areas).
- **Strike risk**: high (large slow surface dwellers), medium (large but agile or partial overlap with lanes), low (agile small cetaceans, deep divers).

## Migration and seasonality

**Year-round residents**: harbour porpoise, killer whale, white-beaked dolphin, long-finned pilot whale (more offshore in winter), Atlantic white-sided dolphin, sperm whale males off Andoya / Bleik shelf edge.

**Arctic / ice associated**: beluga, narwhal, bowhead (year-round in Svalbard or Russian Arctic).

**Summer feeding visitors (Jun-Sep, range Apr-Oct)**: blue whale (peaks around Svalbard, Jan Mayen and off northern Norway), fin whale, sei whale, minke whale, northern bottlenose whale.

**Mixed winter aggregations**: humpback and killer whale form winter feeding aggregations in northern Norwegian fjords (Skjervoy, Kvaenangen, Kaldfjorden depending on year), Nov-Jan, following Norwegian spring-spawning herring. The same humpback individuals feed at higher latitudes in summer (Barents Sea, Svalbard).

## Conservation context

**OSPAR threatened and declining cetaceans (NE Atlantic)**: bowhead, North Atlantic right whale, blue whale, harbour porpoise.

**ASCOBANS** covers all small cetaceans (Odontoceti) in the Baltic, NE Atlantic, Irish and North Seas, except the sperm whale.

**NAMMCO** (Norway, Iceland, Greenland, Faroes) provides species accounts and management advice; not a Red List authority.

## Strike risk classification rationale

Highest risk in Norwegian and surrounding waters, per Laist et al. 2001, Rockwood et al. 2017 and the global Nisi et al. 2024 hotspot analysis:

- **High**: large slow surface-occupying whales. Blue, fin, sei, humpback, sperm whale, North Atlantic right whale (where present).
- **Medium**: minke whale, killer whale, pilot whales.
- **Low**: porpoises, dolphins (agile, bycatch dominates), beaked whales (deep divers, acoustic disturbance dominates), Arctic species mostly north of main shipping lanes.

A 2024 global analysis (Nisi et al., Science) estimated mandatory speed restrictions cover only 0.54% of blue whale collision risk hotspots, 0.27% for humpback whales, and effectively zero for fin and sperm whales.
